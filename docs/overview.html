AI Learner Licence

Grade 7–8 · 25–35 minutes · browser-based

Try itai-learners-license.vercel.app

A short course that teaches students to judge when AI helps their learning, and when it quietly does the learning for them.

What it is

Four modules and a final challenge, finished in one class period. It runs in any browser on a Chromebook, phone or laptop. Nothing to install, no accounts to provision, no licence fee.

Students work through realistic school situations and decide what they would do, rather than memorising a rulebook.

Why it exists

Students are already using these tools. A blanket ban teaches them to hide it; an open door teaches them nothing. Neither builds the skill they need, which is telling help from substitution in the moment.

The course is built on one idea: AI can help you learn, but it should not do the learning for you. The same action can be fine on one assignment and wrong on the next, so the course teaches the judgment call rather than a fixed list of permissions.

What students work through

01 Where's the Line? support vs. replacement
02 Make AI Coach You tutor, coach, practice partner
03 Sounds Right. Check It. verification and bias
04 Know the Boundaries permission, privacy, responsibility
05 Final Challenge 12 mixed situations, 10 needed to pass

Why a pass means something

A mastery check, not a click-through. Each module draws 5 questions from a bank of 8 and needs 4 of 5; every miss must be corrected before the student continues. The final needs 10 of 12, drawn from a bank of 20.

Answer order is shuffled rather than authored, so the correct choice falls evenly across all four positions instead of clustering, and a retry reshuffles it alongside a different set of questions.

The five checks students carry out of the course

  1. Allowed? Is AI allowed for this task, or this part of the task?
  2. Goal? What am I supposed to learn or practise?
  3. Still me? Am I still doing that thinking and making the decisions?
  4. Check it? Does anything AI told me need confirming somewhere reliable?
  5. Private? Am I sharing anything I should keep private?

Five questions, not a policy document. They transfer to any subject and any tool, and a staff could adopt them without adopting the course.

How this is AI literacy

Not prompt-writing. Four transferable judgments, each built by the module beside it.

  • Knowing when a tool fits the task Recognising what a task is meant to teach, and whether a shortcut skips exactly that. Modules 01, 02
  • Evaluating output instead of trusting it Treating a confident answer as a claim to check, and noticing a one-sided one. Module 03
  • Protecting private information Their own, and other people's, which students reliably find harder. Module 04
  • Owning what they submit Understanding that "AI told me" transfers no responsibility. Module 04, Final

Privacy and data

The course collects nothing. No accounts, no sign-in. It never asks for or stores a name, email address, student ID or school account, and does no tracking.

Progress stays in the browser on the device and is never transmitted. The course makes no call to any AI service, so nothing a student types reaches a third party.

What it does not claim

The licence a student earns is a classroom learning credential, not an official school or district certification.

It is not permission to use AI. The licence itself says so, and carries the line "Valid only when AI use is permitted by your teacher." A teacher's instructions for any given assignment always come first.
