# AI Learner Licence — running it with a class

One page for the day you teach it. For setup and code, see `README.md`.

**Link:** https://ai-learners-license.vercel.app

---

## Before the lesson

**Open the link on a student Chromebook, signed in as a student, on student wifi.** Not on your own machine. Staff accounts usually get looser content filtering than student ones, and a division filter that blocks `vercel.app` will look fine from your desk and block all thirty kids at once. This is the most likely thing to derail the lesson, and it takes two minutes to rule out.

Everything else works offline-ish: the course loads once and then makes no network requests at all.

## What students get

A pre-check, four modules, and a final challenge. About 25 to 35 minutes start to finish.

| Stage | What happens |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-check | 5 questions, optional, skippable, not marked |
| Modules 1 to 4 | Teaching, practice, then a 5-question check. 4 of 5 clears it. |
| Corrections | Any question they miss comes back as a quick either/or before they move on |
| Final challenge | 12 situations, 10 of 12 to pass |
| Licence | A printable credential with a number and an issue date |

Modules unlock in order. There is no timer anywhere.

## Say this before they start

> This licence does not mean you are allowed to use AI. It means you can tell when AI is helping you learn and when it is doing your thinking for you. Whether AI is allowed on any assignment is still whatever I told you for that assignment.

The course says this too, on the licence screen and on the licence itself. Saying it out loud first stops the one misreading that matters.

## Shared Chromebooks

Progress saves to the browser profile on that device, not to a student account.

If a student opens the link and sees someone else's progress, they click **Reset progress** in the top right and start clean. Worth saying at the top of each period if devices move between classes.

Two consequences:

- A student who starts in class and tries to finish at home begins again from zero. Plan to finish in one sitting.
- Pressing browser Back leaves the course. Going back to the link picks up exactly where they were, so nothing is lost.

## Things that will come up

**"I failed the final."** That is designed in. They get a different set of situations, not the same ones again, and the course tells them which areas to revisit first. No penalty and no limit on attempts.

**"Can I print it?"** Yes. The licence screen has a Print button. The printout drops the navy background and the rest of the page and gives you the credential alone, so it does not eat a colour cartridge.

**"Is this going on my mark?"** Nothing leaves the device. No account, no sign-in, no name, no data sent anywhere. You see what they did by looking over their shoulder, which for a 30 minute pilot is the better instrument anyway.

**A student finishes in 15 minutes.** They skipped the reading. Ask them to explain one answer back to you.

## After the lesson

Worth noting while it is fresh:

- Where the room went quiet and where it got loud
- Any question where several students argued the wrong answer was right, which usually means the question is ambiguous rather than the students are wrong
- Whether 25 to 35 minutes matched the room
- Anything a student said about the licence that suggests they read it as permission

The course has an exit survey built in. It is switched off for this run, so student answers stay on the device. If you want results collected for a later run, `README.md` has the setup and the privacy trade-offs under "Collecting exit-survey results".
